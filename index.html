01 / Introduction

My story

My journey into technology began in an unexpected place: the classroom.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, teaching moved online, and I found myself creating digital worksheets and virtual learning environments for my students. As I explored these tools, I became increasingly curious about the technology behind them.

I wanted to understand not only what users see on a website, but also what happens behind the scenes. How does a website communicate with a server? How is information stored and retrieved? How do front-end and back-end technologies work together to create a seamless user experience?

That curiosity inspired me to pursue Computer Systems Technology at Saskatchewan Polytechnic, where I am developing skills in programming, web development, databases and software design. My goal is to become a full-stack developer who creates practical, accessible and user-friendly applications.

My background in education shaped how I approach technology. Working with students, educational assistants, parents and social workers taught me to communicate clearly, collaborate with people from different backgrounds and find solutions that meet individual needs.

Outside programming, I enjoy reading classical English literature and exploring interior design. Both encourage creativity, empathy and attention to detail.

03 / Selected work

Project 01 · WebStorm

D&B Musical Emporium

HTMLCSS

I independently developed a multipage website for a fictional musical instrument store, with product listings, business information and a wishlist.

Situation

My coursework required a website that displayed musical products and let visitors navigate between pages and product descriptions.

Task

Build a consistent website, connect the required files and make the product navigation work.

Actions

I structured the pages with HTML, styled them with CSS and checked resource and page links. When product links failed, I reviewed paths and file references repeatedly, corrected the problems and retested the pages.

Results

I delivered a complete 13-page website, including a homepage, business information, wishlist and 10 individual product pages, and earned full marks for the assignment. I identified and corrected broken page and resource paths during development, strengthening my ability to troubleshoot navigation problems independently.

Artifact and reflection

The complete original website demonstrates product listings, detailed product pages, styling and navigation. I'm proud that I worked through broken links independently. Next time, I would establish the folder structure earlier, test links continuously and use a debugging checklist.

Project 02 · IntelliJ IDEA

Hospital Management System

JavaJDBCSQLMicrosoft Access

I developed a Java client-server application supporting the creation, retrieval, updating and deletion of patient records in an academic database.

Situation

I needed to build an application that communicates with a database and performs the four fundamental CRUD operations.

Task

Implement the operations, connect Java to Microsoft Access and demonstrate patient-record management.

Actions

I wrote the client, socket server, request handler and JDBC database methods. Updating records was especially challenging, so I reviewed and revised that code and retested the operations.

Results

I demonstrated the completed application and received a perfect grade. The project supported all four core database operations: creating, retrieving, updating and deleting patient records. Completing the update workflow was a particular achievement and strengthened my understanding of how Java, networking and JDBC work together.

Artifact and reflection

The supplied Java source files show how I separated user interaction, networking and database access. I'm especially proud of resolving the update operation. Next time, I would strengthen input validation, error handling and systematic testing, and explore a browser-based interface.

Project 03 · C# data structures

AVL Tree Implementation in C#

C#RecursionAlgorithms

An Advanced Programming assignment exploring AVL Trees, Binary Search Trees, balance factors, tree rotations (LL, RR, LR, RL), and inorder traversal.

Situation

My data structures coursework explored how ordinary binary search trees can become unbalanced.

Task

Construct an AVL tree, calculate heights and balance factors, perform inorder traversal and identify the correct rotations.

Actions

I studied the theory, practised examples, revised my work and sought instructor guidance, particularly for the more difficult LR and RL double rotations.

Results

I completed a working C# AVL tree project covering all four rotation cases: LL, RR, LR and RL. The implementation and accompanying work demonstrate tree construction, height and balance-factor calculations, and inorder traversal. Revising the two double-rotation cases with instructor guidance strengthened my ability to reason through and debug complex algorithms.

Artifact and reflection

The C# source and rotation worksheet demonstrate my algorithmic reasoning. I'm proud that I persevered through the difficult double rotations. Next time, I would practise more examples, draw clearer diagrams, check balance factors carefully and document my steps in greater detail.
